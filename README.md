Be it enacted by the House of Representatives and the United States Senate in Congress assembled, that:
The purpose of this bill is to establish long-term, stable fuel efficiency standards for motor vehicles sold in the United States — lowering costs for consumers, reducing dependence on foreign oil, and limiting the environmental impact of the transportation sector.
PURPOSE
Purpose & Goals
The purpose of this bill is to establish long term stable fuel efficiency standards for motor vehicles sold in the United States. The goal is lower costs for consumers, reduced dependence on foreign oil, and a smaller environmental impact from the transportation sector.
SEC. 1
Reducing Greenhouse Gas Emissions
This Act reduces carbon dioxide and other greenhouse gas emissions from motor vehicles by improving fleet-wide fuel efficiency, thereby mitigating the effects of climate change and improving air quality.
SEC. 2
Fuel Efficiency Standards
All new vehicles and light trucks manufactured for sale in the United States shall meet a minimum fleet-wide average fuel economy of 40 miles per gallon, as measured by the EPA. This standard shall be phased in over a period of five years, increasing incrementally each model year until the full standard is met.

SEC. 3
Administration & Enforcement
The EPA and the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration shall jointly administer this Act, issue implementing regulations within 12 months of enactment, and establish penalties of $5,000 per vehicle for manufacturers that fail to meet fleet-wide standards in a given model year.
12 MO
Deadline for regulations

SEC. 4
Incentives
The EPA shall establish a tax credit program to encourage early compliance with the efficiency standards set forth in Section 2. Manufacturers that exceed the required fleet-wide fuel economy standard in a given model year shall be eligible for a tax credit of up to $1,000 per qualifying vehicle sold.
$1,000
Tax credit, per qualifying vehicle
SEC. 5
Reporting Requirements
Manufacturers shall submit annual reports to the EPA detailing their fleet-wide fuel economy performance. The EPA shall submit a report to Congress every two years assessing the effectiveness of this Act in reducing greenhouse gas emissions and dependence on foreign oil.
Conclusion
This Act represents a balanced approach to improving the fuel efficiency of the United States motor vehicle fleet. By combining clear standards, reasonable enforcement mechanisms, and incentives for early compliance, this bill aims to reduce consumer fuel costs, strengthen national energy independence, and decrease the environmental footprint of the transportation sector for future generations.
